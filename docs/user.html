LabTrack for lab members

What the lab owns, where it is, who has it, and how to book it for Tuesday. This is everything you need; nothing here requires being an admin.

Getting in

Open the app and click Sign in with Microsoft. Pick your Johns Hopkins account. That is the whole of it — there is no separate LabTrack password to keep.

The one thing that trips people up

Use your JHED sign-in name — jdoe1@jh.edu — not the mail alias @jhu.edu. They look interchangeable and they are not: the lab's roster is keyed to the sign-in name, so the alias produces a successful Microsoft sign-in followed by "not authorized", which looks like something being broken rather than like a typo.

If you are refused, the message names the exact address it saw. Read it — that address is usually the answer. Send it to an admin and ask to be added.

Trying it without signing in

The sign-in page offers three sample identities — Member One, Member Two and Admin. They open a small pretend lab that lives entirely in your browser and never touches the real one. Nothing you do there is real; nothing real leaks in. It is a good way to click around before you have to get anything right.

Your session

Sign-in lasts about an hour and renews itself quietly in the background. If renewal fails you get a dark red bar with Sign In Again. Click it. Finish anything half-typed first — a form still open when the session lapses cannot be saved.

Finding your way around

The padlock

The app opens read-only. The red padlock in the header unlocks editing, and it re-locks every time the page loads — so the first time you click Add Item in a session, it will often just tell you editing is locked. Click the padlock, then click again.

What the padlock is and is not

It guards against a stray click, not against people. It lives in your browser only, it does not travel with your account, and it does not cover everything — checking out, returning, logging a delivery and moving an order along all work while it is locked. Treat it as a lid, not a permission.

Everything updates itself

The app re-reads the lab every 30 seconds, so a colleague's checkout appears on your screen without a refresh. Changes you make are protected from being overwritten for a minute afterwards, so your own edit will not flicker back.

Finding a thing

Search matches the name, the location, the category, the tags, the serial number and the Label ID — so if you are holding the object, type the number on its sticker.

The coloured chips under the search box filter by category, and the dropdown after them sorts. Switch between photo cards and a dense table with the toggle at the right. The list starts at 24 items and loads more as you scroll — in table view the scrolling happens inside the table, which is easy to miss.

Label IDs

Every trackable item carries a short ID printed on a sticker: RM-001 — a per-category prefix and a number. Click the chip on any card to copy it. Where one model has several units they get a suffix each: RM-001-01, RM-001-02. Those are separate items with separate histories, even though they share a name.

Worth knowing

Supplies you consume — solder, gloves, zip ties — get no label. Nothing is stickered, so there is no number to print.

What a status means

StatusMeaning
AvailableOn the shelf. It may still be booked for later — check the calendar before you count on next week.

Maintenance / BrokenSomebody's judgement about the object. Returning something never clears these.
Low Stock / Out of StockA count that has fallen to or below the threshold someone set for it.

Borrowing something

  1. Click the item, then Check Out — or use Check Out in the header to book several things at once.
  2. Pick the dates. If the item is shared you can also give a daily window — 09:00 to 17:00 — and it is yours only during those hours each day.
  3. Add a note if it helps someone find you. Add colleagues' addresses under Group members if they should be able to return it too.
  4. Submit. You will either have it, or you will be told it is waiting for an admin, and why.
Whose name it goes in

Yours. The Person field is filled from your sign-in and the server rewrites it to match, so a booking cannot be filed under someone else by accident — or on purpose. An admin logging a checkout on somebody's behalf is the exception.

Group members

Anyone you list can return the item. That is all it grants — they cannot edit or withdraw your request. Use the same @jh.edu sign-in addresses, comma-separated; capitals do not matter.

Quantity

Not what you would expect

The quantity on a checkout is a note about how many you took. It does not reduce stock and it does not leave the rest for anyone else — the item is booked for those dates either way. If two people genuinely need to hold different units at the same time, they need to be separate tracked units, which an admin can set up.

Why a booking waits

Most bookings go straight through. Two things send one to an admin, and the app tells you which:

ReasonWhat it means
Longer than 7 daysA long hold takes something out of circulation, so somebody looks at it.
Someone is already waitingAnother request is pending for an overlapping slot. Whoever asked first has their claim looked at, rather than being quietly overtaken by a shorter booking.

And three things are refused outright:

RefusalWhy
Already bookedAn active booking owns its dates. This applies to shared items too — sharing means several people over a week, not several people in the same hour.
Too far aheadNothing may start more than 31 days from today.
The dates do not make senseA return before the checkout, a window that ends before it starts, half a window, or a hold longer than 90 days — usually one wrong digit in a year.
While you wait, nothing is held for you

A pending request reserves nothing. Several people may ask for the same week and an admin picks between them. And when yours is approved the clash test runs again — if somebody took the slot in the meantime, approval comes back "taken while this was waiting". It is not lost work; it is the honest answer.

Changing your mind

While a request is still waiting you can Edit it — move the dates or the window, change the group — or Withdraw it, which removes it rather than leaving a rejection on the record. Both are in the Usage tab under Waiting for approval. Once it is approved, editing is closed: return it and book again.

Giving it back

Find the item — or its row in Usage → All Records — and click Return. To do several, tick them and use Bulk Return. Shift-click selects a range.

You can return anything you booked, anything you were listed on as a group member, and an admin can return anything. If the button is missing, the booking is in somebody else's name.

Overdue

Past its return date with nothing given back, an item is overdue — and it stays yours until somebody actually returns it. The clock does not hand it back for you. You will see a red banner on the way in, and the lab gets a reminder each morning.

Supplies you use up

Consumables — solder, gloves, wipes — are not checked out. Their card has a Use button: enter how many you took and the count drops. There is nothing to give back.

Some supplies are not counted at all. Those show Notify instead, which tells whoever restocks that it is running low. Say what you noticed — "down to the last roll" is more useful than a number nobody is tracking.

Adding and correcting things

member You do not need an admin to add something to the inventory, or to fix a record that is wrong. Unlock the padlock and use Add Item, or open an item and click Edit.

You can change what an item is like:

name · category · quantity · unit · location · minimum stock · photo · tags · serial number

Only an admin can change what an item is:

its status · its Label ID · whether it is shared · whether it is a consumable

Those four decide how every rule treats the object, and its Label ID is already on a sticker in the room. If you edit them the app tells you they were left alone rather than pretending to save them.

Adding several of the same model

Set the quantity above 1 and a box appears offering to track each unit individually — it is ticked by default. Ticked, you get five separate items with their own labels and serials, which is what you want for five cameras. Unticked, you get one row with a count, which is what you want for five HDMI cables. If you are not sure: can they be borrowed separately?

Asking to buy something

  1. Order Requests → New Request. One store per request; add as many items to it as you like.
  2. Fill in what it is, how many, a link if you have one, and the price. Say why in the reason box — that is what an admin reads.
  3. Set the urgency honestly. Urgent and High jump the list and are marked in the lab's daily summary.
  4. Submit. It goes in as Pending and an admin decides.
About the price box

Type it however you like — $14.99, 1,000, 10-15, ask Bob. Anything that is a single number is added to the total; anything else is shown to the buyer exactly as you typed it and left out of the arithmetic, with the total saying how many it left out. A range is a range, not a thousand-dollar item.

You can edit or delete your own request while it is still pending. Once an admin has approved it, it is theirs.

The calendar

The Calendar tab shows the lab's bookings by month or by week. A booking spanning several days is drawn as a band across them; a pending request is drawn differently from a confirmed one; deliveries show up too. Today brings you back.

Putting it in your own calendar

Calendar → Subscribe gives you a personal address. Paste it into Google Calendar (Other calendars → + → From URL), Outlook (Add calendar → Subscribe from web) or Apple Calendar (File → New Calendar Subscription). Every booking in the lab then appears in your own calendar and keeps itself up to date.

It is read-only. Nothing you do in your calendar changes anything here.

Treat the address like a key

Anyone you send it to can read the lab's bookings without signing in, and it does not expire. Do not paste it into a channel — send people here to get their own. If it does get out, Subscribe → Replace this address kills the old one immediately.

It is not a live view

Your calendar app decides how often to check, and Google in particular can be several hours behind. It is good for "is the arm claimed next Tuesday" and no use for "is the arm free right now". Come back to the app for that.

Notes you put on a checkout stay in the app and are not carried into the feed — but item names, people's names, dates and locations are. That is the shape of what somebody holding the address can see.
