The five things worth knowing before anything else

  
Sign in with @jh.edu Your JHED sign-in name, not the @jhu.edu mail alias. They look interchangeable; the roster only knows one of them. It is the most common way to be locked out.
The padlock re-locks Every page load. It is a lid against stray clicks, not a permission, and it does not cover checking out or returning.
Nothing is held while you wait A pending request reserves nothing. Approval re-runs the clash test, so a slot can be gone by then.
In Use means somebody has it Not that somebody booked it. A hold for next month sits on the calendar and leaves the thing on the shelf.
The clock never returns anything Past its date with nothing given back, an item is overdue and still yours. Only returning it ends a loan.

Where things actually live

LabTrack is one page in your browser and a Google Sheet behind it. Everything you do writes a row in that Sheet — which is why an admin can always go and look, and why a change that did not stick is a question with an answer rather than a mystery.

ThingLives in
Items, bookings, orders, deliveriesTabs of the lab's Google Sheet
Who is an admin, who is a member, the categoriesThe Settings tab of that Sheet
Who did what, and what was deletedThe AuditLog and DeleteLog tabs
Your calendar subscription addressIssued to you by the app; revocable at any time
Your sign-inMicrosoft, with your Johns Hopkins account. There is no LabTrack password.

Found something wrong here?

These pages are part of the repository, next to the code they describe, so a correction survives. Tell an admin what is wrong and it gets fixed in the same place as the thing it was wrong about.
