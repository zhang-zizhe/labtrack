Running LabTrack

Everything in the member guide applies to you too — an admin is a member with more buttons. This covers the rest.

Two places called Settings

Almost everything here lives in the Settings tab of the spreadsheet — one of the tabs along the bottom of the Sheet, next to Items and Checkouts. Apps Script has a page called Project Settings as well, holding the time zone and the script id, and it holds none of this. Wherever this guide says "the Settings tab", it means the spreadsheet one.

Admins are always members, even if nobody put them on the member list — a lab cannot lock its own admins out by tidying the roster.

Looking at it as a member does

The sign-in page's Admin preview identity shows the app with admin powers against sample data. To see what a member sees, sign in normally and use preview from the header — it drops you to a member's view of the real lab. Nothing is editable and no request reaches the backend, so the Digest and Backup buttons say "preview only" rather than pretending to work.

The Settings tab

Two columns, key and value. Every value must be exactly what its row expects.

KeyWhat it holds
adminsA JSON array of sign-in names: ["jdoe1@jh.edu","asmith3@jh.edu"]
membersThe same shape. Empty means anyone with a JHU account can sign in.
categoriesA JSON array of category names. Edit through the app instead.
cat_prefixesCategory → label prefix. Edit through the app instead.
slack_modeall, important, digest or off
last_backupWritten by the app. Do not edit.
ics_tokensCalendar addresses, one per person. See Calendar addresses.
Valid JSON, or nobody is an admin

admins and members are parsed as JSON. A missing bracket or a smart quote makes the value unreadable, and an unreadable admin list reads as nobody is an admin — after which the only way back is this tab. Keep the square brackets, the double quotes and the commas. Editing the roster through the app is checked and refuses a list that would leave you out; editing the cell by hand is not.

Who gets in

Add a person by putting their sign-in name in members. Make them an admin by putting it in admins.

Sign-in name, not mail alias

jdoe1@jh.edu, not john.doe@jhu.edu. The token carries the sign-in name, so seeding the address you know a colleague by produces a successful Microsoft sign-in followed by "not authorized" — which reads like the system being broken rather than like one wrong word. It is the most common way to lock somebody out.

Someone leaving

Take them out of both lists. That is enough: their sign-in stops, and their calendar subscription stops too, because the feed rechecks the roster on every fetch. Their bookings and their name stay on the record, which is the point of a record.

Deciding whether to fill in members

Left empty, anyone in the university tenant who finds the URL can sign in and start editing the inventory. Filled in, only the people you list can. There is no third option, and it is easier to decide before people are relying on it than to take access away afterwards.

Approving bookings

Requests waiting on you appear at the top of the Usage tab, grouped by item so you can see everyone competing for one thing in one place. Each shows who, which dates, how long, and why it is waiting — longer than 7 days, or somebody was already in the queue.

  1. Read the group, not the request. If three people want the arm next week, the point is to choose.
  2. Approve the one that should have it. The clash test runs again at that moment — if the slot went in the meantime, you get "taken while this was waiting" and nothing changes.
  3. Reject the others. Rejecting is not unkind; leaving them pending forever is.
Approving one does not reject the rest

The others stay pending and simply stop being approvable, because the slot is now taken. The confirmation tells you how many will be left in that state. Clear them.

Nothing is reserved while a request waits, on purpose: a rejected request would otherwise have quietly held an item hostage until you got to it.

Items only you can change

Members can correct anything about what an item is like. Four fields describe what it is, and each changes how every rule treats the object:

FieldWhy it is yours
StatusMaintenance and Broken are judgements about the object that a return must not quietly undo. In Use is derived from bookings, not set by hand.
Label IDIt is already printed on a sticker in the room. Changing it in the app does not change the room.
SharedDecides whether the thing can be booked by the hour or is one person's for the whole range.
ConsumableDecides whether it is borrowed and returned or drawn down and restocked.

When a member edits one of these, the app tells them it was left alone rather than appearing to save it. These are set when the item is created — anyone adding something says what kind of thing it is — and only an admin can change them afterwards.

Splitting an item into units

admin One row with a count of five becomes five rows with their own labels, serials, locations and histories. Open the item, click Edit, set the quantity above 1, leave Track each unit individually ticked, fill in the per-unit details and confirm. The original becomes unit -01.

It is admin-only because splitting has to move the base row's Label ID, and that is a sticker in the room.

Labels and prefixes

The gear at the end of the category chips opens Manage Categories. Add, rename or remove a category, and set the prefix that goes on its stickers.

A prefix defaults to the category's initials — Robots & Motors becomes RM. Numbers count up per prefix, so the first sensor is SV-001 even when the robots are already at RM-004. The number is assigned by the server as the item is saved, so two people adding at once cannot be handed the same one.

Changing a prefix does not relabel anything

Existing items keep their old IDs, which is correct — those stickers are on shelves. You will have two series running in one category until they age out. Choose a prefix once, early.

Removing a category leaves its items behind

They keep the category name but lose their filter chip, so they are reachable only through All or search. Move them first.

Purchase requests

The status a request carries is what tells everyone where it is:

StatusMeans
PendingAsked for. Waiting on you.
ApprovedYou said yes. Nobody has bought it.
OrderedActually purchased. Waiting on a box.
ReceivedIt arrived. Add it to the inventory from here.

Only an admin moves a request between these. A member can edit or delete their own request while it is still pending.

Getting the order out

Select the approved requests and use Copy Text for a list you can paste into an email to whoever buys, or Purchase Summary to build a formatted sheet in the spreadsheet with quantities, prices and a total.

Prices that are not numbers

The price box is free text and people write 10-15 or ask Bob in it. Anything that is a single number is totalled; anything else is shown to you exactly as it was typed and left out of the arithmetic, with the total saying how many it left out. Read that note before you order against the number.

Deliveries

Log Delivery records something that arrived. If it answers an order, use Mark Received on the order instead — it carries the details across and offers to put the thing straight into the inventory, where you give it a location, a label and a serial.

Receiving does not add to a count

It creates a new item, or you skip it. Restocking an existing consumable means editing that item's quantity — the delivery is a record of a box arriving, not an adjustment.

Notifications

Set slack_mode in the Settings tab:

ModeWhat gets posted
allEvery event as it happens.
importantOnly deletions, urgent orders, overdue items and approvals.
digestNothing during the day; it all arrives in the 5pm summary. Anything urgent still goes out at once.
offNothing, including the daily summary.

Capitals do not matter — Off and off are the same. A value the app does not recognise falls back to all rather than to silence, since the only place a wrong value could announce itself is the channel you are trying to quieten.

Everything goes to one channel. There is no per-person routing: nobody is told individually that their order was approved or their loan is late. Say so, or people will assume they will be chased.

What runs on its own

WhenWhat
Every day, 5pmThe summary: orders needing you, overdue items, low stock, the day's activity.
Every day, 8amOverdue reminder, if anything is overdue.
Every day, 6amRe-derives every item's In Use flag from live bookings. Yesterday's bookings ended and today's began while nobody was looking.
Sundays, 3amA copy of the whole spreadsheet.

Digest in the header sends the 5pm summary now — useful before a lab meeting.

Calendar addresses

Each person's Subscribe button issues them their own address, recorded in ics_tokens. You do not need to hand these out and you should not collect them.

The address is the credential

A calendar app cannot sign in — it is fetched by Google's or Microsoft's servers on a schedule, with nobody present — so holding the address is the permission. It does not expire. Anyone it is forwarded to can read the lab's bookings: item names, people's names, dates and locations. No email addresses, and no checkout notes.

Revoking

To revokeDo this
Your ownSubscribe → Replace this address. The old one dies immediately.
One person's, for themAsk them to do the above. If they cannot sign in, an admin can replace it from the Apps Script editor.
Somebody who has leftTake them off members. The feed rechecks the roster every fetch.
Everyone's, at onceClear the ics_tokens value. Each person mints a new one next time they open Subscribe.

Replacing an address is written to the audit log.

The audit trail

Two tabs in the spreadsheet, neither shown in the app:

TabHolds
AuditLogWho did what: items added, edited and deleted, checkouts and approvals, order decisions, settings changes, calendar addresses replaced. Name and verified sign-in address.
DeleteLogWhat was removed, with enough of the record to reconstruct it.

The identity in both comes from the verified sign-in, not from anything the browser claimed. When a non-admin unlocks the padlock that is recorded too.

Backups

A copy of the whole spreadsheet is taken every Sunday at 3am into a folder in the lab account's Drive. Backup in the header takes one now — worth doing before anything bulk.

What a backup does not protect you from

The copies live in the same Drive as the original. They cover a bad edit and a deleted tab. They do not cover losing the account — which is the failure that would take the inventory with it. Share the Sheet with a second person, or pull a copy down now and then.

Keeping the lab account

The Sheet, the backend and the calendar proxy all belong to one Google account, and it is an ordinary consumer account — Johns Hopkins does not run Google Workspace. There is no administrator anywhere who can recover it for you.

Four things, none optional

Point account recovery at the PI, not a student. Turn on two-factor authentication and keep the backup codes wherever the lab keeps shared credentials. Share the Sheet itself with a second person as an editor — the cheap insurance that keeps the data reachable if the account is ever lost. And sign in occasionally: an unused consumer account can be deleted after two years, and since everyone signs in with Microsoft, human sign-ins to this account can be zero for years while the lab uses it daily.

One more, which is a policy question rather than a technical one: the Sheet holds lab members' names and sign-in addresses on a service the university does not administer. It is equipment records rather than research data, but that is the question a compliance review would ask.

When something looks wrong

SymptomWhere to look
Somebody cannot sign inAsk them to read the address in the refusal. Nine times out of ten it is @jhu.edu where @jh.edu belongs. Otherwise check members.
Nobody is an admin any moreadmins is not valid JSON. Fix the brackets and quotes in the Settings tab.
Slack has gone quietCheck slack_mode, then that the webhook is still configured. Both are in the deployment notes, not here.
Slack will not go quietslack_mode must be off. Anything unrecognised means all.
An item reads In Use with nobody holding itUsually an unreturned loan — that is what overdue means. The 6am sweep re-derives every flag; it can be run early from the editor.
A subscribed calendar stopped updatingThe address was replaced, or the person came off members. Their calendar will be showing an event saying the link is no longer valid.
A change did not stickEverything writes to the Sheet. Open it and look at the row. AuditLog says whether the write was attempted.
A blank pageNot something a person can cause. It goes to whoever maintains the code.
Not in this guide, on purpose

Deployment ids, script ids, webhook URLs and subscription addresses are credentials — anything holding one can act without signing in. They live in the deployment notes in the repository, not on a page anyone can open. This guide only ever tells you which row to edit.
